It’s a cultural cliché to bemoan the passing of the monoculture, that bygone era when everyone seemed to watch, read and argue about the same movies and books at the same time. Yet 2026 is proving to be a real throwback, especially if you happen to live in New York: The Knicks won a championship, bringing entire neighborhoods out to watch together in the streets. Then the World Cup descended, drawing millions of simultaneous global eyeballs. Now Christopher Nolan has delivered “The Odyssey,” a film that feels old-fashioned in every sense: epic, unabashedly cinematic and seemingly the topic of every conversation.

Some culture-war sniping started long before the film’s release, which Daniel Mendelsohn, who translated the epic poem, smartly dismissed in a recent guest essay. And as Sharon Waxman noted, the film has sparked discussions that reflect broader cultural tensions.
